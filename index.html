Abstract

Reach is not enough

A mobile robot can reach a task object yet remain poorly positioned for its manipulation policy. Choosing a useful base stance requires understanding the policy’s visual and action preferences, beyond geometric reachability alone. We introduce Stand to Succeed, a policy-aware stance predictor that reuses a pretrained vision–language–action model without adapting its manipulation weights. During training, the policy’s action expert exposes representations at candidate stances. A probe fitted to sparse manipulation outcomes links these representations to success, and a pose-conditioned student predicts them from the robot’s current stereo observation. Feature matching and score preservation transfer candidate-view knowledge to unexecuted stance queries, while a supervised readout complements the fixed probe. At deployment, the predicted success field guides geodesic repositioning through traversable space, and a compact monotone gate decides when to hand off. Experiments on eight articulated-object tasks in held-out kitchens show improved task completion, with representation comparisons and budget studies explaining the contribution of policy-derived supervision.

Method

How it is trained

1Rollout & Distillation

The frozen policy runs from candidate stances; its action expert exposes a feature h at each one.

2Score Match

A probe fitted to the sparse outcomes turns each feature h into a success score.

3Score Predict

From the robot’s current view at a, a pose-conditioned student predicts z ≈ h for every candidate stance.

4Deploy

In a held-out kitchen, the predicted success field guides the robot to a good stance before it hands off.
